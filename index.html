Step 2

Your path

Stage 1 — starting or buying

I want to start or buy a business

Good income, no asset. Every dollar stops the day you stop.

There are only two ways out: build something from scratch, or buy something that already works. Most people assume it has to be the first and never start, because building from zero while holding down a job is brutal.

Buying is the faster path and almost nobody realises it's available to them. You don't need the purchase price sitting in your account. Businesses get bought with the business's own cash flow, with vendor finance, with earn-outs — every week, all over Australia, by people who look exactly like you.

Three myths this kills

  • You need money to make money. You need a structure, not capital.
  • You need industry experience. Not if you buy something already under management with a team in place.
  • It takes years. Not with a process for finding motivated sellers instead of hunting blind.

Before your call, get clear on

  • What you'd want to own — and what you'd never want to own
  • What you could actually deploy in the next 90 days
  • Whether you want to keep your job while you do this (most people do, at first)

If the thought of this makes you nervous, that's the normal reaction, not the exception. Almost everyone further down this page felt it before their first move. What shifts it isn't confidence, it's seeing the actual steps.

Stage 2 — getting out of the middle

I want my time back

Revenue might be fine. Your calendar isn't. You're still the one the business can't run without, which means you don't have a business — you have a demanding job you also happen to be liable for.

Getting to Stage 3 means the business runs on systems and people instead of on you. That's what frees you up to go do the next one. Nobody builds a second business while they're still working in the first.

The second half of it is knowing how to acquire rather than start. Buying a business that complements what you already own is the fastest way to grow.

Before your call, get clear on

  • Your last 12 months' revenue and roughly what falls to the bottom line
  • The three tasks only you can currently do
  • What you'd do with 15 hours a week back

Stage 3 — the next one

I'm out of the day-to-day. What's next?

You've done the hard part, and it puts you in a small group. The business runs, your time is yours, and the question stops being "how do I get free" and becomes "what do I point this at".

From here it's acquisition and portfolio. You've got the one thing that makes buying straightforward: an existing entity with cash flow, a team and a balance sheet to structure against. Deals that are hard for a first-time buyer are considerably easier for you.

The other half is making sure the thing you built keeps running while your attention moves. Plenty of people get to 3, start something new, and quietly get pulled back into the first business within six months.

Before your call, get clear on

  • What the existing business throws off, and what you'd be comfortable deploying
  • Whether you want more of the same industry or deliberately something else
  • Who actually runs the current business if your attention halves
Actually, I'm between things — time and capital, no business right now

Redundancy, a business you've sold, a role you've left, a career break, an early exit. Whatever brought you here, you've got the one thing almost nobody else booking this call has: time.

That's a genuine advantage and it has a shelf life. The risk isn't that you'll fail — it's that six months of research quietly becomes twelve, the runway gets short, and you take a job to stop the bleeding. People in your position move faster than anyone when they have a process, and slower than anyone when they don't.

Acquiring is usually the right route. You can be at meetings on a Tuesday morning when someone holding down a job can't, and sellers respond to buyers who are actually available.

Before your call, get clear on

  • How much runway you've actually got, in months
  • What you could deploy now, and what you'd want to keep untouched
  • Whether you want to run what you buy, or have it run for you from the start

Being between things and being under real financial pressure aren't the same situation. If the runway is thin, say so early on the call. I'd rather tell you honestly that the timing is wrong than watch you commit money you need for something else.

Step 4

Your questions, answered

What actually happens on this call?

We map where you are now, where you want to be in 12 months, and what's genuinely in the way. You'll leave with a plan whether or not you work with us.

If we can help, I'll say so and explain exactly what that looks like. If we can't, I'll tell you that too and we'll part as friends.

What is the investment to work with you?

There are several ways in, and the main options sit at roughly $6K, $10K, $15K and $36K depending on how much coaching and support comes with it. The self-paced end starts at around $6K; the fuller coaching programs sit at the upper end. There are higher levels again for people building a portfolio of businesses. Instalment plans are available on all options.

Which one makes sense depends entirely on where you are and what you're building — that's what we work out on the call, and I won't put you in something bigger than your situation warrants. Come with a rough sense of what you could put toward this in the next 30 days and the conversation gets far more useful.

Do you do it for me, or do I have to do the work?

With you. Every person on this page did their part — the meetings, the calls, the follow-through. We bring the process, the coaching and the accountability; you bring the effort. It only works when both halves show up, so it's worth being honest with yourself before the call about whether you've got the capacity right now.

I'm still employed. Is this too early for me?

It's the right time. Hundreds of Aaron's students have moved from employee to business owner, and several of them are on this page. The plan gets built around your actual situation, including your job.

I've never owned a business. Does that rule me out?

No. Several of the people above had no business background at all — an anaesthetist, a PhD dropout, an employee on $60K who ended up buying the company he worked for. What matters is whether you'll follow a process.

I don't have money to invest in a business.

That's most people on this call. Watch the 36-minute video on your path before we speak — the acquisition structures in there are the reason "no capital" stops being the blocker. The deals above are real examples of it.

Do you guarantee results?

Not an outcome, no — and be wary of anyone who does. The people above are real students who got real results, and they also did the work. Your outcome depends on your situation, your effort, and factors nobody controls.

What is guaranteed is the event itself. Attend day one of Empire Mastery and if you don't feel you've received value worth many times the ticket price, you get a full refund, no questions asked.

How long before I see something happen?

Honestly? It varies enormously, and anyone who gives you a neat number is guessing.

On this page alone: Rumbie did a deal four weeks after learning the process. Sandra found her opportunity on day one and had completed the takeover three months later. Kaine put his together a month after. At the other end, one couple worked a business for four months, got talked out of it during due diligence, lost momentum entirely, and didn't acquire until well over a year later. Both of those are normal.

The difference is almost never talent or capital. It's how many conversations you're willing to have, and how you handle the first one that falls over.

How do I know this will actually work for me?

You don't. I'd be lying if I said otherwise, and you should be suspicious of anyone who tells you it's certain.

What you can do is look at whether people who started where you're starting have got where you want to go. There are a lot of them on this page, across a lot of industries, including plenty with no business background at all. That's evidence. It isn't certainty, and nothing is.

The effort you put in dictates the result you get. Show up, ask questions, do the work, and I don't see a reason you wouldn't see a result. But you'll only ever find out by starting.

Does this only work in Australia?

No. Students have used the same process in Singapore, India, Europe and the United States. The strategy travels — what changes country to country is the legal and structuring detail, and that gets covered directly.

After the training, am I on my own?

No. There's an alumni community of students and coaches where deals, introductions and partnerships genuinely get made, and further levels of support if you want closer guidance. We'll walk through what's available for your situation on the call.

Can I bring my partner?

Please do. If they're part of the decision, they should hear it firsthand rather than secondhand from you at dinner — it's much harder to relay a plan than to sit in on one. Same goes for a business partner. If they genuinely can't make the time, tell me at the start and we'll work around it rather than pretend otherwise.

Is one call enough, or is this a process?

Sometimes one is enough. Often we'll book a second, longer session to go properly granular on your numbers, your situation and what working together would actually look like, line by line.

Something's holding me back — is that normal?

Completely. Almost everyone booking this call has one of the below somewhere in the back of their mind.

One thing worth saying first. The skill this entire thing runs on is deciding. Every person on this page hit a moment where they had incomplete information and moved anyway — that isn't recklessness, it's the job. Owners and investors don't get handed certainty before they act; they act, then build the certainty.

The aim of our call is simple: by the end of it you'll know enough to say yes or no. Both are good outcomes and I'm genuinely fine with either. The only answer that helps neither of us is maybe.

Part of me wants to think about it first

That's fair — and it's the whole reason this page exists.

By the end of our call you'll have seen the results, the process, the timeframes and the numbers. There won't be a piece of information sitting behind a curtain that you need to go away and find. So if you notice yourself wanting more time afterwards, be honest with yourself about whether it's really information you're short of.

Because "thinking about it" feels like the neutral option and it isn't. It's choosing another few months of exactly what you've got right now. Sometimes that genuinely is the right call — just make sure you're choosing it rather than drifting into it.

I honestly don't have the time

You probably don't. Nearly everyone booking this call is already stretched.

But follow it through. Presumably you want this to be something that lasts, not a burst that fades. And realistically, you'll be busy again in the future. Busy doesn't disappear as you grow a business; it usually intensifies.

Which means the best time to learn how to build while you're busy is while you're busy. If you only ever learn it under perfect conditions, the first time life gets loud again is the first time you fall off. And that's exactly the moment you'd want support the most.

It isn't effortless — it's real hours in the early months. But "when things calm down" is a date that never quite lands in the diary.

I've been burned by a program before

A lot of people on this call have. It's a fair reason to be sceptical of me and of Aaron, and I'd be more worried if you weren't.

The useful question isn't whether the last one worked, it's what specifically was missing. Nine times out of ten it's one of three things: nobody held you accountable, the content was generic instead of applied to your actual business, or there was no one to ask when you got stuck.

One bad experience is information. It isn't a verdict on every mentor you'll ever meet — and letting it become one costs you far more than the program did.

I'm not sure I can afford it

That's a normal place to be, and it usually splits two ways.

Sometimes the money genuinely isn't there. Say so plainly on the call and I'll be straight with you about what's actually possible. More often it could be found, and it's the certainty that can't — that isn't really a money conversation, it's a belief conversation, and it's a far more useful one to have out loud than to sit on.

One line I'll hold either way: don't borrow money you can't comfortably service. That isn't how any of the stories on this page started.

What if it doesn't work for me?

Then it doesn't, and that's a real possibility — which is why nobody here will promise you an outcome.

Think of it like hiring a personal trainer. Best program, best nutrition plan, best gym in the city — they still can't turn up for you. We control the process, the coaching, the accountability and the fact it has worked repeatedly for people starting exactly where you're starting. You control execution. That split is identical with any coach, in any field, at any price.

So the real question is which of the two you'd be inside it: the person who joins and waits for it to work, or the person who joins and does the work.

Becoming the person who actually does this

This is the one nobody puts on a page, and it's the real one.

Whoever you are today built the life you currently have. That isn't a criticism, it's just arithmetic. The person who gets to where you want to go makes different decisions, has conversations they'd currently avoid, and sits with a level of discomfort they don't sit with now.

Call it you 1.0 and you 2.0. The trap is waiting to feel like 2.0 before you start acting like them — waiting to feel ready, confident, qualified. It runs the other way round. You take the actions first, badly and nervously, and the identity catches up afterwards. The couple who quit two secure jobs with no plan B. The anaesthetist who'd never bought anything. The bloke who thought the whole thing was a cult and came anyway.

None of them felt ready. They went first and became the person afterwards.

So the honest question before our call isn't whether you can afford it or whether the timing is perfect. It's whether you're willing to be uncomfortable for a while in exchange for a different result. If the answer is no, that's worth knowing too — just be certain it's an actual no, and not a not-yet that never arrives.
